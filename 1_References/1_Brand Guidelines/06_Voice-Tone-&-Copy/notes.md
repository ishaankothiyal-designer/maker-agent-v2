## Tone of Voice — Core Principles
*"These core principles define how Cars24 sounds across every market, every channel, every interaction."*

### Confident, not loud.
*"Clarity earns trust. Volume does not."*
- We know the pre-owned car market. That should come through in what we say, not how loudly we say it.
- If it only works at high volume, it's too loud.

### Warm, not familiar.
*"Care, not closeness."*
- We speak with care, not as the reader's best friend.
- Think of a knowledgeable colleague, not a stranger trying too hard at a party.

### Sharp, not clever.
*"Say something worth saying."*
- The right word, the right insight, the right amount.
- No puns, no wordplay for its own sake, no copy more interested in sounding smart than being useful.

## Universal Guidelines (common across all markets — non-negotiable)

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Be human and empathetic. Every person you're speaking to is making a big life decision v/s simply ordering groceries. | Use jargon, robotic language, or brochure-speak. |
| Lead with what the customer gets, not what Cars24 does. | Make Cars24 the subject. ("We offer...", "We are the only...") |
| Back every claim with a specific: a number, a fact, a feature. | Use empty superlatives. ("Best prices!", "Biggest selection!", "Unbeatable!") |
| Be confident. Own what we do without needing to shout about it. | Declare trust with adjectives. ("100% Trusted!", "Hassle-free!") Prove it instead. |
| Guide the customer to a natural next step. Never pressure them. | Use urgency or countdown language unless justified (for sales & limited-time offers). |
| Act like a knowledgeable colleague, not a best friend. | Be over-familiar. |

## Guidelines — India
*India carries the widest emotional register. The tone can be affirming, celebratory, and bold.*

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Adapt tone to the moment: upbeat for wins, calm and solution-focused for problems. | Mistake "positive" for "overhyped". Celebrate real things, don't invent superlatives. |
| Celebrate customer milestones with warmth. | Exaggerate the customer's experience. Big brand claims are fine; inflated personal claims are not. |
| Make the solution feel effortless. Confidence comes from ease, not force. | Forget the purpose. Every bold statement should tie back to solving something for the customer. |

---

## Guidelines — UAE
*The UAE audience is global (200+ nationalities), discerning, and ad-literate. Trust is earned through specificity and restraint.*

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Write for a global room. 200+ nationalities. Be sharp, not simplified. | Simplify for a reader who doesn't exist. Global doesn't mean lowest common denominator. |
| Set the scene; let the reader supply the emotion. ("The Eid morning drive, windows down.") | Prescribe emotions. ("Feel the joy of driving a Cars24 car this Eid!") |
| Write at an elevated register. This is a discerning market. Considered word choices signal quality before the car even enters the conversation. | Assign buyers an identity. ("Perfect family car!", "Your wife will love it!") |

---

## Guidelines — Australia
*Understatement, restraint, and honest wit do the job that volume cannot.*

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge risk openly. Honest reassurance is more convincing than false confidence. | Over-promise. One specific claim is worth ten sweeping ones. |
| Use understatement. Restraint earns more trust than enthusiasm. | Use dealership language. |
| Allow subtle, observational wit where it fits naturally. | Sound like you're trying too hard. Australian cultural currency is effortlessness. |
| Run the Aussie filter: would an Aussie trust this? | Be loud, flashy, or self-congratulatory. Low ego wins, always. |

## Copy & Grammar Guidelines
*"Clear, consistent, and human communication is central to the Cars24 brand. All copy should reflect a tone that is simple, confident, and customer-first."*

1. **All standard rules of grammar apply.**

2. **We follow British spellings.**
   - Correct: colour, favourite, recognised, tyre
   - Incorrect: color, favorite, recognized, tire

3. **Sentence case, not title case.**
   - Correct: Sell your car today
   - Incorrect: Sell Your Car Today

4. **Punctuation with punctuation, or no punctuation.**
   - Where a headline has a punctuation mark in the middle of the phrase, there must be a concluding punctuation mark at the end. If there is no punctuation in the middle of the phrase, there is no punctuation at the end.
   - Correct: "Got a car? Sell it in 24 hours." / "Sell your car in 24 hours"
   - Incorrect: "Got a car? Sell it in 24 hours" / "Sell your car in 24 hours."

5. **Our name is Cars24.**
   - The brand name should always be written as "Cars24." Uppercase C, lowercase ars, no space before 24.
   - Correct: Cars24
   - Incorrect: CARS24, cars24, Cars 24, CARS 24

6. **Date format.**
   - We follow: date, month, year, where applicable. "th", "st", "rd", "nd" are always superscript. We use a comma between the month and the year.
   - (Example reuses Cars24 / CARS24 vs cars24, Cars 24, CARS 24 styling.)

7. **Oxford comma.**
   - When listing multiple items, we always use the Oxford comma.
   - Correct: Inspected, certified, and ready to drive.
   - Incorrect: Inspected, certified and ready to drive.

### Prices follow the currency and number format of the market.

**India**
- Prices are written in lakhs and crores, not in plain numbers.
- Correct: Starting at ₹3.5 lakh. — Incorrect: Starting at ₹350,000. / Starting at Rs. 3,50,000.
- "Lakh" and "crore" are lowercase in running text. The ₹ symbol is always used.
- Avoid "Rs." or "INR" in consumer copy.
- Monthly instalment format (India): From ₹X,XXX/month

**UAE**
- Prices are written in AED, using the international numbering system (thousands, not lakhs).
- Correct: AED 45,000. Starting at AED 12,500. — Incorrect: Dhs 45,000. AED 45K.
- "AED" always precedes the number, with a space between the abbreviation and the figure.
- Correct: AED 45,000 — Incorrect: 45,000 AED
- Monthly instalment format (UAE): From AED X,XXX/month

**Australia**
- Prices are written in $, using the international numbering system (thousands, not lakhs).
- Correct: $18,500. Starting at $9,990. — Incorrect: AU 18,500. A$18,500.
- The $ symbol precedes the number with no space.
- Correct: $18,500 — Incorrect: $ 18,500
- Monthly instalment format (Australia): From $X,XXX/month

## Creative Direction Insight
Cars24's voice is the voice of a knowledgeable friend who happens to know everything about cars. They're not trying to sell you — they're genuinely helping you decide. The brand never shouts. When it needs to be bold, it uses specificity and clarity, not volume or superlatives. In creative executions, this means headlines should feel like something a smart person actually said — not like an ad headline.
